I love my family

Talk about relatives, age, work and possessive adjectives.

Lucy introduces her family

Hello! I’m Lucy. I’m 30 years old, and I live in London. I have a small family, and we are very close. I have one brother. His name is Daniel. He is 43 years old, so he is much older than me. He works in London too.

My parents still work. My father is 62 years old. He is a businessman and has a transport company. My mother is 60 years old. She has a clothes shop in London. She likes fashion and meeting people.

I also have grandparents. They are retired now. My grandfather is 82 years old, and my grandmother is 79 years old. They live near London. I love my family. We are different ages, but we like spending time together.

B
Countries · Cultures

Tell me about your country

Read conversations with Kenneth, Natalia and Alicia, then describe traditions.

United States

🇺🇸 Kenneth

“I’m from the United States, and I’m proud of my country. Thanksgiving is an important celebration. Families meet, eat together and give thanks. On the Fourth of July, people watch fireworks and have picnics. Baseball, basketball and American football are popular. Many people enjoy barbecues and road trips. We have people from many cultures.”

Russia

🇷🇺 Natalia

“I’m from Russia. My favourite celebration is New Year. Families decorate a tree, prepare food, give presents and celebrate together. I also like Maslenitsa. People eat blini and welcome spring. We often drink tea together. In summer, many families spend time at a dacha, a house outside the city.”

Spain

🇪🇸 Alicia

“I’m from Spain. I love our traditions because they bring people together. Family is important, and we like long meals together, especially at weekends. Every region has its own food, festivals and traditions. I like local fiestas, music and street celebrations. I also enjoy learning about other cultures.”

Where & when · BE or DO?

Where are you from? → I’m from Spain. Where does Lucy live? → In London.

When do they celebrate New Year? → In winter. When is the party? → On Saturday.

Use am/is/are when the main verb is be; use do/does with other verbs. He, she, it → does.

Speak about your country

Describe a special celebration. Use: People celebrate… / Families usually… / On weekends… / In summer… / In winter…

C
Dialogue · Everyday actions

Everyday verbs

Mark is investigating Lucy’s daily routine. Learn what she does.

Mark asks Natalia about Lucy

Mark: Natalia, you know Lucy well, right?

Natalia: Yes, I do. Why?

Mark: I need some information about her. What does she do every day?

Natalia: She works in her studio. She paints and draws.

Mark: Does she read a lot?

Natalia: Yes, she does. She likes books and art.

Mark: Does she write too?

Natalia: Sometimes. She writes notes about her paintings.

Mark: Does she walk around London?

Natalia: Yes, she does. She walks a lot and looks at people, streets and buildings.

Mark: Does she travel?

Natalia: Sometimes, but she usually stays in London.

Mark: Does she meet many people?

Natalia: Yes. She meets artists and tourists every day.

Mark: And… does she have a boyfriend?

Natalia: No, she doesn’t. (She smiles.)

Mark: It’s part of the investigation!

Natalia: Of course, Mark. Very professional.

Daily routines · He / She / It

Lucy paints, draws, reads and walks.

Usually: read → reads, walk → walks. After consonant + y: study → studies. Other endings: go → goes, watch → watches.

Question: Does Lucy paint? Yes, she does. Does she travel every day? No, she doesn’t.

D
Grammar

The Present Simple

Build statements, negatives and questions correctly.

Affirmative

I / you / we / they + base verb

I read. They work.

He / she / it + verb -s/-es/-ies

Mark works. Lucy studies.

Negative

don’t + base verb: I don’t work here.

doesn’t + base verb: She doesn’t work here.

Not: She doesn’t works here.

Questions and short answers

Do you read? — Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.

Does Lucy paint? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn’t.

Where does Mark work? — He works in London.

After does, always use the base form: Does she work? (not works).

BE

Is Mark a detective?

Where are you from?

Use am/is/are for descriptions and identity.

DO / DOES

Does Mark work in London?

Where do you live?

Use do/does for other verbs and actions.

Remember: Where is Lucy from? is correct, but Where does Lucy live? is also correct. The auxiliary depends on the main verb.

E
Culture

Family and everyday life

Explore family routines in English-speaking countries.

Families are important in many English-speaking countries. Families can be big or small, and every family is different. In the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia, many adults work during the day. Children usually go to school from Monday to Friday. After work or school, families often spend time together at home.

People cook, eat, talk, watch TV, listen to music or play games. Some go for a walk, visit relatives or go to a park. Weekends are often important: people visit parents or grandparents, meet friends, play sports or travel.

In Britain, drinking tea is a famous tradition, although British people also drink coffee. In the United States, family barbecues are popular, especially in warm weather. Family traditions are different for everyone because English-speaking countries have many cultures, languages and celebrations. There is no single “English-speaking family”. Every family has its own story.
